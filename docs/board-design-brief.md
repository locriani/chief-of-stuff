## The one constraint

No information leaves the page. Every field the tracker holds is on the board today and stays on it. Density is to be solved by structure — hierarchy, grouping, progressive disclosure, consistent shapes — not by omission.

## What the page is

One HTML page, generated by `scripts/render_board.py` from the day's tracker and log, published as a claude.ai Artifact after every tracker edit, read by Zach on a laptop and a phone. Sections in order today: **Sessions** (a tree of session nodes with facts), **Unassigned** (table), **Zach's queue** (table), **Final requirements** (checklist with counts), **Today** (Gantt strip: lanes on a clock axis, calendar bands, deadline line), **Week** (same, seven days), **Lanes** (the full table, grouped running / orphaned / open · waiting / done, each item expandable to its history).

## Where the structure is inconsistent (the diagnosis to test, not the answer)

- **The item cell is three things at once.** A name, a history (`**22:20: handed to research-1 …**` appended each move), and a status note, all in one Markdown cell. The renderer splits it into a summary and a `hist` list by sentence, which is why 92 names overflow and 50 start with asterisks. The coordinator already writes a bold headline as the lane's short name; nothing reads it as one.
- **Three tables show lanes three ways.** Unassigned (item · due · state), Zach's queue (item · due · state), Lanes (item · owner · state · since · due · size). Same rows, three shapes, two of them subsets of the third.
- **Two strips show the same lanes at two scales**, each with its own fold rule and its own summary label grammar (`no estimate, or due or estimated after today` vs `N due · M est.` vs `→ Final`).
- **Sessions is a tree of `<dl>` facts; Lanes is a table; requirements are a checklist** — three disclosure idioms on one page, each with its own indent, type size and open/closed convention.
- **Everything is closed at rest.** 123 disclosures and none open means the first screen is summaries of summaries; what needs attention has no place to surface except colour.
- **The order of sections is the order they were built in**, not the order of a reading: the thing Zach checks first (what is blocked, what moved, what is due) has no section.

## What is already decided (do not reopen)

- Flat fills, one hue per state, no gradients or patterns (0.9.1, findings 51 → 69). Tests assert pairwise-distinct fills and no `gradient` anywhere.
- No caption prose on the session graph (0.9.1).
- A fold opens onto positioned sub-rows on the same axis, not a text list (0.10.0, `_bar_row`).
- Lanes may carry a `size` column and `done HH:MM–HH:MM`; estimates come from per-size history with a labelled queue-drain fallback (0.11.0, findings 77–78). The size is a coordinator judgement in a column, never inferred from prose.
- The board is never hand-written: whatever the design, `render_board.py` produces it from the tracker, and the tracker grammar in `agents/chief-of-stuff.md` (`## Tracker`) is the only input.

## Questions only Zach can answer

1. **What is the first screen for?** Blocked lanes, what moved since he last looked, what is due next, or the whole state? The answer decides what is open at rest.
2. **May the renderer treat a leading bold phrase in an item as the lane's name**, drawing the rest as history — or should the tracker grammar gain a `name` the coordinator writes, like `size`?
3. **One lane table or three?** Unassigned and Zach's queue could be filters or groups of the one table.
4. **Two strips or one with a zoom?** Today and Week share every row.
5. **Phone or laptop first?** 1100px and 26px rows were chosen for neither.
